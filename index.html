Lesson phrases

Short bebop-style walking-bass phrases, written in C. Pick a key, press Practice, and the phrase loops with piano and drums behind it. Everything else (speed, loop, mixer, Listen scoring) works as usual.
The idea in brief

Bebop scales are ordinary scales with one extra chromatic passing tone, which makes eight notes. In steady quarter notes, eight notes fit two bars, so the chord tones keep landing on beats 1 and 3 whether you go up or down.

Dominant bebop: 1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7 7 (Mixolydian + natural 7).
Major bebop: 1 2 3 4 5 ♯5 6 7 (major + ♯5).
Minor (Dorian) bebop: 1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7 7 (Dorian + natural 7).

Good lines mix these scale runs with arpeggios (root–3rd–5th–7th), chromatic approaches (a half step above or below the next chord's root, on beat 4) and enclosures (a note above and a note below the target). The target is usually a chord tone of the next chord, on beat 1.

Other books and teachers sometimes add a different passing tone to the minor bebop scale (a natural 3rd). This app uses the natural 7, because it keeps the chord tones on beats 1 and 3 when descending from the root too.

Generate a walking bass line

Type a chord chart (bars separated by |, two chords in a bar separated by a space) or pick a preset. You get a bass line plus piano and drums to play along with. Transpose or loop it like any other song.

How to use Bass Tutor

The idea

Learn jazz bass lines by seeing exactly where to put your fingers: the fretboard shows the current note and what’s coming, the tab highway scrolls the line in time, and the notation view shows the same bars on a bass-clef staff (written an octave above the sound, as bass is).

Songs

  • Add song (MIDI): load any .mid file and pick the bass track. The other tracks (and drums) play as backing. Pick a different track to practice any other line.
  • Lessons: bebop scales, arpeggios with approach notes, enclosures, a turnaround and a blues phrase. Pick a key and press Practice; the phrase loops with a rhythm section. Turn on all 12 keys to move up a fourth every pass.
  • Generate walking line: type changes (e.g. Dm7 | G7 | Cmaj7) and get a walking, two-feel or bossa line with piano and drums. New variation rolls a different line over the same chords; Export MIDI saves it.
  • Chords for imported files are guessed from the other tracks, so check them and fix them in the Chords box.

Practising

  • Loop & speed: click a bar for the loop start, a second bar for the end. Slow it down; the line stays in time.
  • Keys: Transpose, or have the loop move up a fourth every pass to practise all 12 keys. Fingering is recalculated for each key.
  • Mixer: mute the bass to play it yourself, or hear only the bass to study it.
  • Marker label: show finger numbers, note names or chord degrees (R, 3, 5, ♭7…) to see how the line outlines the changes.
  • Fingering is chosen by an optimiser that minimises shifting; if you’d play a note elsewhere, pause and click it in the tab highway to cycle its position.

Listening (optional)

Turn on Listen and play your bass; the app detects the pitch and scores notes as Perfect / Good / OK / Missed / Wrong. Use Calibrate latency once for your setup. Wait for me pauses the music until you play each note.

Shortcuts

Space start/pause · Esc stop · V switch view · L loop · [ ] slower/faster · M mute bass

Notes

Tempo and time-signature changes inside a MIDI file are not followed (the first ones are used). Works best in Chrome or Edge. Songs you add are stored only in this browser.
